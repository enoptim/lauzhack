# Who the twin is for

## Main character: Tom Watts (made up)

Tom Watts is in charge of energy for this building, the one where the hackathon takes place. He leads a small team of engineers. His boss, the finance director, decides what gets money.

- **Finance director:** says yes or no. Wants proof the upgrade pays back. "I can't approve a guess."
- **Tom:** buys our tool. Wants his team's ideas approved, fast.
- **Engineers:** use our tool every day. Want to test five ideas in five minutes.

## Before and after

**Before:** an idea needs weeks of spreadsheets. The finance director asks "How sure are you?" Tom can't answer, and the idea dies.

**After:** Tom tries five options with the finance director in the room. They see energy, comfort and money side by side. They decide in the meeting.

## Why this is true

- Good ideas rarely fail for lack of ideas. They fail because nobody trusts the savings, and money is tight ([study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.erss.2018.08.008), [ENERGY STAR](https://www.energystar.gov/buildings/facility-owners-and-managers/industrial-plants/make-business-case/engage-upper-management)).
- In one study of homes, the promised savings were 2.5 times bigger than the real savings ([study](https://haas.berkeley.edu/energy-institute/research/abstracts/wp-261/)). So our twin starts from the building's real energy use.
- From Enoptim's customer talks:
  - An engineer at a large customer said the one thing they want is a digital twin of their buildings.
  - A senior manager only got interested when we showed savings in money, not kilowatt-hours.

## Ending: zoom out

Tom has one building. A UBS property fund has hundreds. "Which one do we fix first?" UBS's Swiss property funds want to halve their CO₂ by 2030 ([UBS](https://www.ubs.com/ch/en/assetmanagement/capabilities/real-estate/sustainability/rethinking-energy-efficiency.html)).

**Weak spot:** we have never talked to a finance director.
